2. Check a car before you call the seller

Data comes live from the U.S. government's NHTSA vehicle database. A clean recall result does not replace a paid history report or an inspection.

Red flags in a listing
  • Price far below similar cars, with a story about why it must go fast
  • Seller will not meet in person, only wants wire transfer, gift cards or crypto
  • Seller refuses to share the VIN or photos of the title
  • Title brands such as salvage, rebuilt, flood or lemon-law buyback
  • Odometer reading that does not match the wear on pedals, seat and steering wheel
  • Photos that look like stock images, or a different location than the seller says
  • "Curbstoner" signs: several cars for sale from one seller, and the name on the title is not theirs
Before you pay
  • Run the VIN here, then consider a full history report (Carfax or AutoCheck)
  • Pay a local mechanic for a pre-purchase inspection (typically $100–200)
  • Meet in a public place; for private sales, complete the transaction at a bank or DMV office
  • Confirm the title is in the seller's name and has no lien
  • Is the title clean, and is it in your name?
  • Has the car been in any accidents or had major repairs?
  • Do you have service records?
  • Why are you selling it?
  • Any warning lights, leaks, or problems with the A/C, brakes, or transmission?
  • How many owners has it had?
  • Can I take it for a test drive and to my mechanic for an inspection?
  • Is the price firm?
